Heart Disease Prediction Using Machine Learning¶

Project Objective¶

The objective of this project is to develop a machine learning model that predicts whether a patient has heart disease using clinical and demographic information. Through exploratory data analysis, data preprocessing, and model comparison, this project aims to identify the factors most associated with heart disease and evaluate which machine learning algorithm provides the best predictive performance.

Evaluation¶

Logistic Regression was the best-performing model, achieving an accuracy of 85.2%, an F1-score of 87%, and a recall of 88% for detecting patients with heart disease. The confusion matrix showed that the model correctly identified 29 patients with heart disease and 23 patients without heart disease. It incorrectly predicted heart disease for 5 patients and missed 4 patients who actually had heart disease. Since this is a medical prediction problem, a high recall score is important because it means the model was able to identify most patients who may have heart disease.

The Decision Tree model achieved a similar recall of 88%, but it had lower overall accuracy due to a higher number of false positive predictions. Random Forest, K-Nearest Neighbors, and Support Vector Machine models showed lower performance on this dataset, which may be due to the relatively small dataset size and the need for further model tuning.

Conclusion¶

This project developed machine learning models to predict the presence of heart disease using patient health information from the Cleveland Heart Disease dataset. Multiple classification models were evaluated, including Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, K-Nearest Neighbors, and Support Vector Machine.

Among the models tested, Logistic Regression achieved the best overall performance with an accuracy of 85.2%, a recall of 88%, and an F1-score of 87%. The model was able to correctly identify most patients with heart disease, making recall an important metric for this healthcare prediction task.

Feature importance analysis showed that variables such as oldpeak, slope, thalach, and restecg had the greatest influence on the model's predictions. Although age and cholesterol are commonly recognized risk factors for heart disease, they had a smaller impact on the model's predictions in this dataset. This may be because other heart-related measurements provided stronger patterns for distinguishing between patients with and without heart disease.

Overall, the results demonstrate that machine learning can be used to identify patterns associated with heart disease risk. However, the dataset used in this project was relatively small, which may limit how well the model performs on new patient populations. Future improvements could include using a larger dataset, adding more patient information, and further optimizing the models to improve prediction performance.
